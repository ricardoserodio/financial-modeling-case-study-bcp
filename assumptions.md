# Assumptions

This file documents the main assumptions used in the simplified financial model for **Millennium bcp / Banco Comercial Português**.

The assumptions are used for educational and portfolio purposes only. They do not represent investment advice, financial advice, internal company guidance or official forecasts.

---

## Model Period

The model uses the following structure:

| Period | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 2022A | Actual | Reported historical year |
| 2023A | Actual | Reported historical year |
| 2024A | Actual | Reported historical year |
| 2025A | Actual | Reported historical year |
| 2026E | Estimate | Forecast year |
| 2027E | Estimate | Forecast year |
| 2028E | Estimate | Forecast year |

Actual figures should be based on public sources, primarily annual reports, financial statements and official earnings presentations.

Forecast figures should be clearly marked as estimates.

---

## Forecasting Approach

The forecast is based on a simplified driver-based approach.

The purpose is not to predict the exact future performance of the bank, but to demonstrate financial modeling logic, banking analysis and assumption discipline.

The model should include three scenarios:

| Scenario | Description |
|---|---|
| Conservative | Lower profitability, weaker revenue growth, higher cost pressure or higher credit risk |
| Base | Moderate continuation of current trends based on historical performance and public information |
| Optimistic | Stronger profitability, better efficiency, lower credit risk or stronger revenue growth |

---

## Net Interest Income

Net interest income is one of the most important revenue drivers for a bank.

Forecast assumptions should consider:

- historical net interest income trend;
- interest rate environment;
- loan book evolution;
- deposit cost evolution;
- asset yield evolution;
- funding cost pressure;
- management commentary from public sources.

Possible modeling approach:

| Scenario | Assumption Logic |
|---|---|
| Conservative | Lower growth or mild decline in net interest income |
| Base | Stable or moderate growth based on recent trend |
| Optimistic | Stronger resilience or higher growth supported by volume and margin trends |

---

## Fees and Commissions

Fees and commissions represent non-interest income.

Forecast assumptions should consider:

- historical fee income trend;
- client activity;
- payments and cards;
- asset management;
- insurance distribution;
- banking services;
- market conditions.

Possible modeling approach:

| Scenario | Assumption Logic |
|---|---|
| Conservative | Low growth or pressure on commissions |
| Base | Moderate growth in line with recent trends |
| Optimistic | Stronger growth supported by higher client activity |

---

## Other Operating Income

Other operating income may include items that are more volatile or less recurring.

Forecast assumptions should be conservative and clearly documented.

Possible approach:

- avoid assuming strong recurring growth from non-core items;
- separate recurring and non-recurring items where possible;
- avoid over-reliance on volatile gains.

---

Operating costs are important for efficiency analysis.

Forecast assumptions should consider:

- inflation;
- staff costs;
- administrative costs;
- technology investment;
- branch network evolution;
- efficiency initiatives;
- historical operating cost trend.

Possible modeling approach:

| Scenario | Assumption Logic |
|---|---|
| Conservative | Higher cost growth and weaker efficiency |
| Base | Moderate cost growth |
| Optimistic | Better cost control and efficiency gains |

---

## Cost-to-Income Ratio

The cost-to-income ratio measures operating efficiency.

It should be reviewed as a consequence of revenue and cost assumptions.

Formula:

Cost-to-income = Operating costs / Operating income

The model should check whether the forecast cost-to-income ratio is realistic compared with historical levels and public management commentary.

- historical impairments;

| Scenario | Assumption Logic |

## Equity and Capital

Equity and capital assumptions should be linked to:

- net income generation;
- dividends or payout;
- retained earnings;
- capital ratios;
- risk-weighted assets;
- regulatory requirements.

The CET1 ratio should use the official reported figure for historical years.

Forecast CET1 assumptions should be conservative and clearly marked as estimates.

---

## Dividend and Payout Assumptions

Dividend assumptions should only be included if supported by public information.

If included, the model should clearly distinguish between:

- reported historical dividends;
- assumed future payout ratio;
- illustrative forecast dividend.

The project must avoid presenting dividend assumptions as a recommendation or forecast guarantee.

---

## Valuation Assumptions

The valuation section may use simplified banking valuation logic, including:

- Price-to-Book;
- Price-to-Earnings;
- dividend yield, if applicable;
- sensitivity to ROE;
- sensitivity to cost of equity;
- sensitivity to growth;
- comparison with selected peers, if relevant.

Valuation assumptions must be presented as illustrative and educational.

The model must avoid language such as:

- buy;
- sell;
- hold;
- target price;
- guaranteed upside;
- investment opportunity.

---

## Key Risks to Assumptions

The main risks to the assumptions include:

- interest rate changes;
- funding cost pressure;
- credit quality deterioration;
- macroeconomic slowdown;
- regulatory changes;
- capital requirements;
- competitive pressure;
- geopolitical or market volatility;
- execution risk;
- changes in international operations.

---

## Assumption Review Checklist

Before finalising the model, each assumption should be checked against the following questions:

- Is the assumption clearly documented?
- Is it based on historical data, public information or explicit scenario logic?
- Is it too optimistic?
- Is it too conservative?
- Is it consistent with the rest of the model?
- Does it affect multiple line items?
- Is the forecast clearly separated from actual historical data?
- Is the conclusion supported by the assumptions?
- Does the wording avoid investment advice?

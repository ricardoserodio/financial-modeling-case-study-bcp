# Investment Memo

## Millennium bcp / Banco Comercial Português  
### Educational Financial Modeling Case Study

This memo summarises the key findings, analytical structure and valuation logic of the financial modeling case study on **Millennium bcp / Banco Comercial Português**.

This document is for educational and portfolio purposes only and does not constitute investment advice.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Millennium bcp is a listed Portuguese banking group with significant public financial disclosure, making it suitable for a public-source banking analysis and financial modeling case study.

This project analyses the bank using publicly available information, including annual reports, financial statements, investor presentations and market disclosures.

The case study focuses on:

- historical financial performance;
- banking profitability;
- operating efficiency;
- asset quality;
- capital strength;
- loans and customer deposits;
- valuation multiples;
- scenario analysis;
- source validation and financial data quality.

The objective is not to produce an investment recommendation, but to demonstrate practical financial modeling, banking analysis, valuation logic and public-source data validation.

---

## 2. Business Overview

Millennium bcp operates as a universal banking group.

Its core activities include:

- customer lending;
- deposit gathering;
- payment services;
- cards;
- asset management;
- insurance distribution;
- corporate and retail banking;
- international banking operations.

The Group has a strong presence in Portugal and relevant international operations, including Poland and Mozambique, with additional exposure to Angola through equity-accounted participation.

The bank’s business model is primarily driven by:

- net interest income;
- fees and commissions;
- loan book evolution;
- deposit base stability;
- cost efficiency;
- credit quality;
- capital generation;
- regulatory capital requirements.

## 4. Profitability Analysis

Profitability is assessed through income generation and returns on equity/assets.

Key indicators include:

- net interest income;
- fees and commissions;
- net income;
- return on equity;
- return on assets;
- earnings per share, where available.

The analysis should focus on whether profitability is improving, stable or declining over time, and whether the trend is supported by recurring revenue drivers or affected by non-recurring items.

For a bank, profitability should not be analysed in isolation. It must be reviewed together with asset quality, capital strength and cost of risk.

---

## 5. Efficiency Analysis

Efficiency is mainly assessed through the cost-to-income ratio.

The cost-to-income ratio compares operating costs with operating income and helps evaluate how efficiently the bank converts revenue into operating profit.

The analysis should consider:

- operating cost trends;
- inflationary pressure;
- staff and administrative costs;
- digitalisation and technology investment;
- revenue growth versus cost growth;
- potential non-recurring effects.

A lower cost-to-income ratio generally indicates better efficiency, but conclusions should be supported by multi-year evidence and public disclosures.

---

## 6. Asset Quality Analysis

Asset quality is essential in banking analysis because credit risk can materially affect profitability and capital.

The analysis reviews:

- non-performing loan or exposure ratio;
- impairments and provisions;
- cost of risk;
- coverage ratios, where available;
- loan book evolution.

The objective is to understand whether the loan book appears stable, improving or deteriorating based on public information.

The analysis should avoid unsupported conclusions and should clearly distinguish reported figures from calculated metrics.

---

## 7. Capital and Balance Sheet Analysis

Capital strength is reviewed through regulatory and balance sheet indicators.

Key indicators include:

- CET1 ratio;
- total capital ratio, where available;
- risk-weighted assets;
- equity;
- total assets;
- loans to customers;
- customer deposits;
- loan-to-deposit ratio.

For banks, capital analysis is central because it affects resilience, dividend capacity, regulatory flexibility and valuation.

The CET1 ratio should be based on official reported figures rather than manually reconstructed calculations, unless all regulatory inputs are clearly available.

---

## 8. Valuation Overview

The valuation approach is simple, educational and based on banking-relevant multiples.

The analysis may include:

- price-to-book;
- price-to-earnings;
- dividend yield, if applicable;
- peer comparison, if relevant;
- scenario-based valuation;
- sensitivity analysis.

For banks, price-to-book is particularly relevant because book value and return on equity are central to valuation logic.

The valuation should be presented as an illustrative exercise rather than a recommendation.

The project must avoid language such as:

- buy;
- sell;
- hold;
- target price;
- investment opportunity;
- guaranteed upside.

Preferred language includes:

- illustrative valuation range;
- educational valuation exercise;
- scenario-based analysis;
- sensitivity-based approach;
- subject to limitations;
- not investment advice.

---

## 9. Scenario Analysis

The model uses three simplified scenarios:

| Scenario | Description |
|---|---|
| Conservative | Lower profitability, weaker revenue growth, higher cost pressure or higher cost of risk |
| Base | Moderate continuation of current trends based on public information |
| Optimistic | Stronger profitability, better efficiency, lower cost of risk or stronger revenue growth |

The purpose of the scenario analysis is to test how different assumptions affect the financial model and valuation outputs.

No scenario should be presented as the expected or guaranteed outcome.

## 11. Data Quality and Source Validation

This project includes a source validation and data quality layer.

The validation process includes:

- mapping figures to public sources;
- checking units and reporting periods;
- separating reported figures from own calculations;
- reviewing ratio formulas;
- checking consistency across annual reports, presentations and financial statements;
- documenting missing values;
- applying a human review checklist before publication.

This strengthens the project by demonstrating not only financial analysis skills, but also awareness of data quality, model risk and AI finance evaluation standards.
